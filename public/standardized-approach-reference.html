📋 Standardized Approach Overview

What is the Standardized Approach?

The Standardized Approach is a simpler method for calculating capital requirements for credit risk under Basel III. Instead of using internal models (like AIRB), banks use external credit ratings from approved agencies to determine risk weights.

Key Characteristics

  • Rating-Based: Uses external ratings (AAA to D)
  • Fixed Risk Weights: Determined by lookup tables, not formulas
  • Simple Calculation: RWA = Exposure × Risk Weight
  • No Internal Models: Doesn't require PD/LGD calibration
  • Automatic Applicability: No regulatory approval needed
  • Collateral Support: Can reduce exposure through haircuts

✓ Basel III Compliant: Standardized Approach is one of two main credit risk methodologies approved under Basel III alongside AIRB.

When to Use Standardized Approach

Banks typically choose Standardized Approach when:

  • They don't have sufficient historical data for AIRB calibration
  • Portfolio is small or simple
  • They want simplicity over precision
  • They're in transition to AIRB
  • Regulatory approval for AIRB is pending

🔐 Collateral Treatment & Credit Risk Mitigation

Collateral Adjustment Formula

When collateral secures an exposure, it reduces the effective exposure through haircut application:

Adjusted Collateral Value:
Adjusted Collateral = Collateral Value × (1 - Haircut)

Adjusted Exposure:
Adjusted Exposure = Loan Amount - Adjusted Collateral

Standard Haircuts by Collateral Type

Collateral Type Standard Haircut Reason for Haircut
Cash 0% Perfect liquidity, no price risk
Government Securities 5% Very low credit risk, low price volatility
Corporate Bonds (IG) 10% Investment grade, moderate volatility
Equities 20% Higher volatility, market risk
Other Collateral 25% Conservative default haircut

Collateral Adjustment Example

Loan with Real Estate Collateral

Loan Amount: $1,000,000
Collateral: Government Securities
Collateral Value: $300,000
Haircut: 5%

Calculation:
Adjusted Collateral = $300,000 × (1 - 0.05) = $285,000
Adjusted Exposure = $1,000,000 - $285,000 = $715,000

Impact: Exposure reduced by $285,000 (28.5%)

⚠️ Important: Collateral cannot reduce exposure below zero. The adjusted exposure must always be ≥ 0.

⚙️ Core Calculation Formulas

Basic Formula (No Collateral)

Step 1: Lookup Risk Weight
Risk Weight = Table Value based on (Rating, Category)

Step 2: Calculate RWA
RWA = Exposure × Risk Weight

Step 3: Calculate Capital Requirement
Capital Required = RWA × 8% (Basel III minimum)

Example: $1M exposure, BBB+ rating (75% weight)
RWA = $1,000,000 × 0.75 = $750,000
Capital = $750,000 × 0.08 = $60,000

Complete Formula (With Collateral)

Step 1: Calculate Adjusted Exposure
Adjusted Exposure = Exposure - [Collateral Value × (1 - Haircut)]

Step 2: Lookup Risk Weight
Risk Weight = Table Value based on (Rating, Category)

Step 3: Calculate RWA on Adjusted Exposure
RWA = Adjusted Exposure × Risk Weight

Step 4: Calculate Capital Required
Capital Required = RWA × 8%

Regulatory Minimum Capital Ratios

Capital Type Minimum % Formula
Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) 4.5% RWA × 0.045
Tier 1 Capital 6.0% RWA × 0.06
Total Capital 8.0% RWA × 0.08

These are minimum regulatory requirements. Banks typically maintain capital above these levels for safety and regulatory comfort.

📐 Worked Examples: Complete Calculations

Example 1: Simple Corporate Loan (No Collateral)

Scenario

Loan Details:
• Loan ID: SA001
• Borrower: Tech Innovations Inc
• Exposure: $2,000,000
• Rating: A (S&P)
• Category: Corporate
• Collateral: None

Step-by-Step Calculation

Step 1: Lookup Risk Weight
Rating: A (Corporate) → Risk Weight = 50%

Step 2: Calculate RWA
RWA = $2,000,000 × 0.50 = $1,000,000

Step 3: Calculate Capital Required (8%)
Capital = $1,000,000 × 0.08 = $80,000

Step 4: Calculate Capital Ratios
• CET1 (4.5%): $1,000,000 × 0.045 = $45,000
• Tier 1 (6.0%): $1,000,000 × 0.06 = $60,000
• Total (8.0%): $1,000,000 × 0.08 = $80,000

Summary

Results:
• Risk Weight: 50%
• RWA: $1,000,000
• Capital Required: $80,000
• Capital Ratio: 4.0% of exposure

Example 2: Secured Corporate Loan (With Collateral)

Scenario

Loan Details:
• Loan ID: SA002
• Borrower: Manufacturing Corp
• Exposure: $5,000,000
• Rating: BBB+ (Moody's)
• Category: Corporate
• Collateral: Government Securities
• Collateral Value: $1,500,000
• Haircut: 5%

Step-by-Step Calculation

Step 1: Calculate Adjusted Exposure
Adjusted Collateral = $1,500,000 × (1 - 0.05) = $1,425,000
Adjusted Exposure = $5,000,000 - $1,425,000 = $3,575,000

Step 2: Lookup Risk Weight
Rating: BBB+ (Corporate) → Risk Weight = 75%

Step 3: Calculate RWA on Adjusted Exposure
RWA = $3,575,000 × 0.75 = $2,681,250

Step 4: Calculate Capital Required
Capital = $2,681,250 × 0.08 = $214,500

Impact Analysis

Without Collateral: $5,000,000 × 0.75 = $3,750,000 RWA
With Collateral: $3,575,000 × 0.75 = $2,681,250 RWA
Savings: $1,068,750 RWA (28.5% reduction)
Capital Saved: $85,500

Example 3: Sovereign Exposure

Scenario

Details:
• Exposure: $10,000,000
• Counterparty: Central Government
• Rating: AAA
• Collateral: None

Calculation

Rating: AAA (Sovereign) → Risk Weight = 0% (Special case!)
RWA = $10,000,000 × 0% = $0
Capital Required = $0

Note: AAA-rated sovereign exposures have 0% risk weight, meaning no capital is required under Standardized Approach.

📊 Standardized vs AIRB Comparison

Aspect Standardized Approach AIRB Approach
Risk Parameters External ratings only Internal PD, LGD, EAD models
Complexity Simple (lookup tables) Complex (formulas & models)
Risk Sensitivity Medium (rating-based steps) High (continuous function)
Data Required External ratings only 5-7 years historical data
Regulatory Approval Not required Required
Calculation Speed Very fast Fast
Capital Efficiency Lower (conservative) Higher (optimized)
Typical RWA 5-150% 5-300%+
Best For Simple portfolios, smaller banks Large banks, complex portfolios

When to Use Each Approach

Choose Standardized Approach if:

  • You have limited historical data
  • Your portfolio is simple and small
  • You want simplicity and ease of implementation
  • You're awaiting AIRB regulatory approval
  • You manage primarily externally-rated exposures

Choose AIRB if:

  • You have 5+ years of historical default data
  • You want to optimize capital requirements
  • You have large, complex portfolios
  • You need fine-grained risk differentiation
  • You have regulatory approval

🏛️ Regulatory Compliance & Basel III

Regulatory Framework

  • Basel III: Global regulatory framework established 2010-2019
  • CRR/CRD IV: EU implementation of Basel III
  • Dodd-Frank Act: US implementation
  • Local Regulators: Central banks in each country add requirements

Standardized Approach Requirements

Requirement Description
External Ratings Must use ratings from approved ECAI (S&P, Moody's, Fitch, etc.)
Risk Weight Tables Must use Basel III prescribed risk weights
Collateral Treatment Haircuts must follow Basel III standards
Capital Minimum 8% of RWA (total capital)
Tier 1 Capital 6% of RWA minimum
CET1 Capital 4.5% of RWA minimum
Regulatory Reporting Must report RWA and capital ratios quarterly

Documentation Requirements

Banks using Standardized Approach must maintain:

  • Documentation of rating sources and dates
  • Collateral values and haircuts applied
  • RWA calculations
  • Capital adequacy calculations
  • Audit trail for regulatory examination

✓ No Model Validation Needed: Unlike AIRB, Standardized Approach doesn't require internal model validation or backtesting because risk weights are prescribed by regulators.

💡 Key Insights & Best Practices

Advantages of Standardized Approach

  • ✅ Simple: Easy to implement and understand
  • ✅ Fast: Quick calculations (spreadsheet-based possible)
  • ✅ No Approval: Can be used immediately
  • ✅ Transparent: Risk weights are public and consistent
  • ✅ Lower IT Cost: Doesn't require complex systems

Limitations of Standardized Approach

  • ⚠️ Less Precise: Rating categories are broad
  • ⚠️ Higher Capital: Often requires more capital than AIRB
  • ⚠️ Rating Dependent: Can only be used for rated exposures
  • ⚠️ Fixed Weights: Can't reflect individual risk characteristics

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • ❌ Using non-ECAI ratings (must be from approved agencies)
  • ❌ Applying excessive haircuts (must follow Basel III standards)
  • ❌ Missing collateral documentation
  • ❌ Incorrect risk weight lookup (verify rating & category)
  • ❌ Forgetting to include all exposures
