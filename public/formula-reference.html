🏦 AIRB Approach Overview

What is AIRB?

The Advanced Internal Ratings Based (AIRB) approach is a method for calculating capital requirements for credit risk under Basel III regulations. Banks use internal models to estimate key risk parameters rather than relying on external ratings.

Key Components

The AIRB framework requires estimation of four key parameters:

Parameter Code Description Range
Probability of Default PD Likelihood of borrower default within 1 year 0.03% to 100%
Loss Given Default LGD Percentage loss on exposure if default occurs 0% to 100%
Exposure at Default EAD Total value of exposure at time of default Any amount
Maturity M Time remaining until loan maturity 0.25 to 5 years

📌 Basel III Compliance: This calculator implements the Basel III AIRB standard formula with proper correlation adjustments, maturity factors, and scaling constants as per regulatory requirements.

📊 Probability of Default (PD)

Definition

The PD is the estimated probability that a borrower will default within a one-year horizon. It represents the likelihood of credit loss.

Key Characteristics

  • Expressed as a percentage (e.g., 1.5%)
  • Minimum value: 0.03% (Basel III regulatory floor)
  • Based on historical default experience
  • Must be calibrated using at least 5-7 years of data
  • Typically increases with credit rating deterioration

PD by Credit Rating

Credit Rating Typical PD Range Risk Category
AAA/Excellent 0.03% - 0.10% Minimal
AA/Very Good 0.10% - 0.25% Very Low
A/Good 0.25% - 0.50% Low
BBB/Satisfactory 0.50% - 1.00% Moderate
BB/Acceptable 1.00% - 2.00% Higher
B/Watch 2.00% - 5.00% High
Below B 5.00% - 100% Very High

PD Calibration

Banks must calibrate PD based on:

  • Historical default rates in the portfolio
  • Forecast of future default rates
  • Business cycle adjustments
  • Stress scenarios
  • Portfolio homogeneity checks

Rule-Based PD Estimation Model

For quick PD estimation without extensive historical data, we provide a rule-based model that uses key financial metrics:

→ Open Interactive PD Calculator

Rule-Based PD Formula:
PD (%) = Base Rate + Leverage Impact + Profitability Impact +
          Liquidity Impact + Coverage Impact

Where:
• Base Rate = 2.0% (long-term average default rate)
• Leverage Impact = (Debt-to-Equity Ratio) × 0.8
• Profitability Impact = Max(0, -EBITDA Margin% × 0.15)
• Liquidity Impact = Max(0, (1.5 - Current Ratio) × 3.0)
• Coverage Impact = Max(0, (4.0 - Interest Coverage) × 0.5)

Model Components Explained

Component Definition Impact on PD
Base Rate (2.0%) Historical average default probability Starting point - increases by other factors
Leverage Impact Higher debt-to-equity = higher risk D/E of 1.0 adds 0.8% to PD
Profitability Lower EBITDA margin = higher risk -10% margin adds 1.5% to PD
Liquidity Lower current ratio = higher risk Ratio below 1.5 adds to PD
Coverage Lower interest coverage = higher risk Coverage below 4.0x adds to PD

Risk Thresholds

PD Range Risk Level Recommendation
0% - 2% Very Low Excellent credit - Standard terms
2% - 5% Low Good credit - Standard terms
5% - 15% Medium Acceptable - Enhanced monitoring
15% - 30% High Elevated risk - Strong covenants
30%+ Very High Significant risk - Restrict or decline

Worked Example

ABC Manufacturing Corp - PD Calculation

Input Metrics:
• Debt-to-Equity Ratio: 0.8
• Interest Coverage: 3.5x
• EBITDA Margin: 18%
• Current Ratio: 1.2

Calculation:
Base Rate = 2.00%
Leverage Impact = 0.8 × 0.8 = 0.64%
Profitability Impact = Max(0, -18 × 0.15) = 0.00% (positive margin)
Liquidity Impact = Max(0, (1.5 - 1.2) × 3.0) = 0.90%
Coverage Impact = Max(0, (4.0 - 3.5) × 0.5) = 0.25%

Total PD = 2.00 + 0.64 + 0.00 + 0.90 + 0.25 = 3.79%

Interpretation: Low Risk - approximately 3.79 in 100 probability of default within one year

When to Use Each Method

Method Use Case Pros Cons
Rule-Based Quick screening, pricing, portfolio review Simple, transparent, repeatable Less precise, generic weights
AIRB Model Regulatory capital, large portfolios Precise, regulatory-approved Complex, requires 5+ years data

Key Assumptions

  • Model calibrated for medium to large corporate exposures
  • Financial metrics are annual figures
  • Assumes normal economic conditions
  • Time horizon: 1 year
  • Weights are based on industry standards (not borrower-specific)
  • Qualitative factors should supplement quantitative results

Model Limitations

  • Based on historical patterns - may not predict sudden changes
  • Assumes continuous business operations
  • Does not capture one-time events, fraud, or management changes
  • Should be updated regularly with new data
  • Industry and geography specific adjustments may be needed
  • Cannot replace thorough credit analysis and due diligence

💰 Loss Given Default (LGD)

Definition

LGD is the percentage of exposure that is not recovered in the event of default. It depends on collateral quality, seniority, and recovery processes.

Key Characteristics

  • Expressed as a percentage (e.g., 45%)
  • Minimum value: 0% (fully secured)
  • Maximum value: 100% (unsecured, no recovery)
  • Depends on loan type and collateral
  • Must reflect economic downturn conditions (PD > LGD floors)

LGD by Loan Type

Loan Type Collateral Typical LGD
Mortgage Real Estate 20% - 35%
Auto Loan Vehicle 25% - 45%
Equipment Financing Equipment 30% - 50%
Corporate Loan Mixed/Various 40% - 60%
Unsecured Personal None 70% - 100%

Factors Affecting LGD

  • Collateral type: Real estate, equipment, cash, receivables
  • Collateral coverage: Loan-to-value (LTV) ratio
  • Seniority: Senior vs. subordinated claims
  • Recovery process: Speed and costs of recovery
  • Economic conditions: Collateral values in downturns

📈 Exposure at Default (EAD)

Definition

EAD is the total value of the loan or exposure if the borrower defaults. It includes principal, accrued interest, and any potential future drawdowns.

EAD Components

  • Principal Outstanding: Amount of loan borrowed
  • Accrued Interest: Interest due but not yet paid
  • Commitment Value: Undrawn credit facilities
  • Other Fees: Prepayment penalties, late fees if applicable

EAD Calculation Examples

Example 1: Term Loan

Loan Amount: $1,000,000
Outstanding Balance: $850,000
Accrued Interest: $12,500
EAD = $850,000 + $12,500 = $862,500

Example 2: Credit Line

Credit Limit: $500,000
Amount Drawn: $300,000
Undrawn (50% assume drawdown): $100,000
EAD = $300,000 + $100,000 = $400,000

⏰ Maturity Factor (M)

Definition

The maturity factor represents the time remaining until loan maturity. It affects the risk adjustment through the maturity adjustment factor b.

Key Characteristics

  • Measured in years (e.g., 3 years)
  • Minimum: 0.25 years (quarterly)
  • Maximum: 5 years (Basel III cap)
  • Longer maturity increases risk weight
  • Applies same adjustment across rating categories

Maturity Adjustment Formula

The maturity adjustment factor b is calculated as:

Maturity Adjustment Factor:
b = 0.11852 - 0.05478 × ln(PD)

Where ln(PD) = natural logarithm of PD as decimal

Impact on RWA

Maturity (Years) Impact on RWA Explanation
1 year Base case No maturity adjustment (M=1)
2-3 years +5% to +10% Moderate increase for medium term
4-5 years +15% to +25% Significant increase for long term

Note: The maturity adjustment increases RWA for longer maturities due to increased credit deterioration risk over time.

🔗 Correlation Adjustment

Definition

The correlation factor represents the degree of co-movement between borrower defaults and systemic (market-wide) economic conditions. It adjusts for concentration risk.

Basel III Correlation Formula

Correlation (R):
R = 0.03 + 0.12 × (1 - e^(-50×PD))

Subject to: R ≤ 0.999

Key Characteristics

  • Range: 3% to 99.9% (capped at regulatory maximum)
  • Increases with PD (higher risk → higher correlation)
  • Lower PD → lower correlation (less systemic risk)
  • Formula ensures inverse relationship with credit quality

Correlation Examples

PD (%) Correlation (R) Interpretation
0.03% (AAA) 3.04% Minimal systemic risk
0.10% (AA) 3.42% Very low systemic risk
0.50% (A) 6.21% Low systemic risk
1.00% (BBB) 10.13% Moderate systemic risk
2.00% (BB) 15.81% Higher systemic risk
5.00% (B) 27.94% Substantial systemic risk
10.00% 42.22% Very high systemic risk

⚙️ RWA Calculation (Core Formula)

Complete AIRB Formula

Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA):
RWA = EAD × [PD × LGD × (1 + (M-1) × b) / (1 - 1.5 × b) × 1.06 - PD × LGD] × 12.5

Formula Breakdown

Step 1: Calculate Maturity Adjustment

Maturity Adjustment = (1 + (M-1) × b) / (1 - 1.5 × b)

This adjusts the risk weight based on time to maturity. Longer maturities increase the adjustment factor.

Step 2: Calculate Risk Weight Component 1

Component 1 = PD × LGD × Maturity Adjustment × 1.06

This is the main risk-weighted loss component. The 1.06 factor adds a 6% safety margin per Basel III.

Step 3: Calculate Risk Weight Component 2

Component 2 = PD × LGD

This is subtracted from Component 1 to remove the base loss (no adjustment case).

Step 4: Calculate Scaling Factor

The scaling factor 12.5 = 1 / 0.08 (inverse of 8% minimum capital requirement)

Risk Weight (%) = [Component 1 - Component 2] × 12.5 × 100

Step 5: Calculate RWA

RWA = EAD × Risk Weight

⚠️ Important: All percentages must be converted to decimals for calculation (e.g., 1.5% = 0.015)

🏛️ Capital Requirement

Minimum Capital Requirement

Tier 1 Capital Required (8% of RWA):
Capital Required = RWA × 0.08

Basel III Regulatory Minimums

Capital Type Minimum Requirement Calculation
Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) 4.5% RWA × 0.045
Tier 1 Capital 6.0% RWA × 0.06
Total Capital 8.0% RWA × 0.08

Capital Buffer Requirements

Beyond minimum capital, Basel III also requires additional buffers:

  • Capital Conservation Buffer: 2.5% (to avoid penalties on dividends)
  • Countercyclical Buffer: 0% - 2.5% (varies by country and cycle)
  • Systemic Risk Buffer: Additional 1% - 3% for systemically important banks

📌 Total Requirement: Minimum 8% + Conservation 2.5% = 10.5% minimum comfortable level (excluding countercyclical and systemic buffers)

📐 Worked Example: Complete Calculation

Loan Details

Parameter Value
Loan ID L001
Borrower ABC Manufacturing Corp
Exposure at Default (EAD) $1,000,000
Probability of Default (PD) 1.5% = 0.015
Loss Given Default (LGD) 45% = 0.45
Maturity (M) 3 years

Step-by-Step Calculation

Step 1: Calculate Maturity Adjustment Factor (b)

b = 0.11852 - 0.05478 × ln(0.015)
ln(0.015) = -4.200
b = 0.11852 - 0.05478 × (-4.200)
b = 0.11852 + 0.23008
b = 0.3486

Step 2: Calculate Correlation (R)

R = 0.03 + 0.12 × (1 - e^(-50 × 0.015))
e^(-50 × 0.015) = e^(-0.75) = 0.4724
R = 0.03 + 0.12 × (1 - 0.4724)
R = 0.03 + 0.12 × 0.5276
R = 0.03 + 0.0633
R = 0.0933 (9.33%)

Step 3: Calculate Maturity Adjustment in Formula

Adjustment = (1 + (3-1) × 0.3486) / (1 - 1.5 × 0.3486)
Adjustment = (1 + 2 × 0.3486) / (1 - 0.5229)
Adjustment = (1 + 0.6972) / 0.4771
Adjustment = 1.6972 / 0.4771
Adjustment = 3.5603

Step 4: Calculate RWA

Component 1 = 0.015 × 0.45 × 3.5603 × 1.06
Component 1 = 0.0255

Component 2 = 0.015 × 0.45
Component 2 = 0.00675

Difference = 0.0255 - 0.00675 = 0.01875

RWA = $1,000,000 × 0.01875 × 12.5
RWA = $234,375

Step 5: Calculate Capital Requirement

Capital Required = $234,375 × 0.08
Capital Required = $18,750

CET1 Required (4.5%) = $234,375 × 0.045 = $10,547
Tier 1 Required (6.0%) = $234,375 × 0.06 = $14,063

Summary

Input: $1M loan, 1.5% PD, 45% LGD, 3-year maturity

Output:

  • Risk-Weighted Assets: $234,375
  • Total Capital Required: $18,750
  • Risk Weight: 23.44%

🏛️ Regulatory Compliance Notes

Basel III Requirements

  • PD Minimum: 0.03% (3 basis points) for corporates, banks, and sovereigns
  • LGD Minimum: No specific minimum, but must be conservative and reflect economic downturns
  • Historical Data: Minimum 5 years for PD and LGD calibration
  • Stress Testing: Models must be stress tested for various scenarios
  • Backtesting: Annual backtesting of PD and LGD estimates required

Validation Requirements

  • Independence of risk assessment from commercial pressure
  • Sound model governance and documentation
  • Regulatory review and approval before implementation
  • Regular monitoring and validation
  • Transparent reporting to regulators

Risk Weights Under AIRB

The AIRB approach typically produces risk weights between:

  • 20% - 35%: High-grade corporate loans (AAA to A range)
  • 35% - 75%: Medium-grade corporate loans (BBB to BB range)
  • 75% - 150%: Lower-grade corporate loans (below BB)

Note: Risk weights depend on all four factors (PD, LGD, EAD, M) and cannot be determined from PD alone.

🔧 LGD Rule-Based Auto-Calculation Model

Overview

The LGD Rule-Based Model automatically calculates Loss Given Default using borrower characteristics, collateral information, and loan seniority. This approach eliminates manual LGD entry and ensures consistency across the portfolio.

Three-Step Calculation Process

Step 1: Base LGD by Seniority & Security Type

The initial LGD is determined by the loan's seniority and collateral type:

Loan Structure Base LGD (%) Rationale
Senior Secured (Real Estate) 30% High recovery from tangible assets with stable values
Senior Secured (Financial Assets) 35% Good recovery, slightly more volatile than real estate
Senior Secured (Other) 40% Reasonable recovery from diverse collateral
Senior Unsecured 45% Limited collateral, relies on borrower assets
Subordinated 65% Junior claim; minimal recovery after senior creditors

Step 2: Collateral Coverage Adjustment

Adjust base LGD based on how well collateral covers the exposure:

Coverage Ratio (Collateral / Exposure) Adjustment (%) Interpretation
> 1.5x −10% Excellent coverage; significant loss mitigation
1.0x to 1.5x −5% Good coverage; meaningful loss mitigation
< 1.0x 0% Undercollateralized; no additional benefit

Step 3: Sector-Specific Adjustment

Account for industry-specific recovery characteristics:

Industry/Sector Adjustment (%) Reason
Real Estate −5% Tangible assets, good recovery history
Financial Services +5% Complex recovery; intangible assets; regulatory risk
All Others 0% No additional sector risk

Formula

Final LGD = Base LGD + Coverage Adjustment + Sector Adjustment

Subject to: Min = 5%, Max = 90%

Worked Example: ABC Manufacturing

Inputs:

  • Loan Type: Senior Secured (Equipment collateral)
  • Exposure Amount: $1,000,000
  • Collateral Value: $600,000 (equipment)
  • Industry: Manufacturing

Calculation:

  • Base LGD (Senior Secured Other): 40%
  • Coverage Ratio: $600,000 / $1,000,000 = 0.6x (< 1.0x) → Adjustment = 0%
  • Sector (Manufacturing): 0% (not Real Estate or Financial)
  • Final LGD: 40% + 0% + 0% = 40%

Result: ABC Manufacturing receives an LGD of 40%, reflecting reasonable recovery potential given the equipment collateral but limited coverage ratio.

Key Assumptions

  • Model calibrated for corporate loans
  • Collateral values reflect normal market conditions
  • Adjustments are marginal not multiplicative
  • Model does not account for correlations between factors
  • Results should be reviewed for reasonableness vs. historical defaults

Model Limitations

  • Fixed weights may not reflect specific borrower situations
  • Does not account for collateral volatility in stress scenarios
  • Recovery processes may be slower than assumed
  • Concentration risk not explicitly modeled
  • Should be complemented by qualitative judgment

⏱️ Maturity Assignment Rules

Overview

Basel III AIRB calculations require a maturity parameter that captures the time horizon of credit risk exposure. The Maturity Assignment Rules auto-suggest maturity based on loan type, with the ability to override for special cases.

Loan Type to Maturity Mapping

Loan Type / Purpose Suggested Maturity (Years) Description
Working Capital / Overdraft 1.0 Short-term liquidity facility; typically renewed annually
Short-term Trade Finance 0.5 Trade credit, import/export financing; very short tenor
Revolving Credit Facility 2.0 Credit lines; longer commitment but flexible draw
Term Loan (Short) 3.0 Standard amortizing loan; medium-term maturity (default)
Term Loan (Long) 5.0 Infrastructure, real estate, project finance
Bond / Note 5.0 Capital market instruments; longer maturity

Maturity Adjustment in AIRB Calculations

The maturity parameter (M) is used in the AIRB risk-weight formula to adjust for term risk:

Maturity Adjustment Factor = (1 + (M − 2.5) × 1.5 × b) / (1 − 1.5 × b)

where b = (0.11852 − 0.05478 × ln(PD))²

Impact: Longer maturity loans receive higher risk weights due to extended credit exposure. The adjustment is most pronounced for lower-PD borrowers.

When to Override Auto-Assignment

  • Covenant Review Dates: If loan has mid-term reviews, use review date as effective maturity
  • Revolving with Bullets: Large balloon payment at end → use final maturity
  • Evergreen Facilities: Indefinite term → use 5 years (regulatory maximum)
  • Bridge Financing: Temporary short-term → use actual expected maturity
  • Letters of Credit: Typical 1-year tenor regardless of underlying facility

Basel III Constraints

  • Minimum Maturity: 0.25 years (3 months) for very short-term instruments
  • Maximum Maturity: 5 years (regulatory cap for AIRB)
  • Maturity Floor: No loan should have maturity less than its actual tenor
  • Rounding: Round to nearest 0.25 year (3-month increments)

Example: Maturity Impact on RWA

Scenario: Same borrower, different loan structures

  • Borrower PD: 2%
  • LGD: 45%
  • EAD: $1,000,000

Loan A (Working Capital, M=1 year): RW ≈ 34% → RWA ≈ $340,000

Loan B (Term Loan, M=3 years): RW ≈ 38% → RWA ≈ $380,000

Difference: +$40,000 RWA due to longer maturity (+11.8%)

Key Assumptions

  • Maturity reflects contractual tenor, not expected payoff
  • Rules reflect Basel III standard for corporate loans
  • Assumes no early repayment (conservative)
  • Adjustments are additive, not multiplicative

📋 Standardized Approach Overview

The Standardized Approach is a simpler, more conservative methodology for calculating credit risk capital requirements. It uses external credit ratings and fixed risk weight tables instead of internal models.

Key Characteristics

  • Rating-Based: Uses external ratings (AAA to D) from recognized rating agencies
  • Fixed Risk Weights: Determined by lookup tables based on rating and exposure category
  • Simple Calculation: RWA = Exposure × Risk Weight (no complex formulas)
  • No Internal Models: Doesn't require PD/LGD calibration
  • Automatic Applicability: No regulatory approval needed
  • Collateral Support: Can reduce exposure through haircut adjustments

When to Use Standardized Approach

  • Banks without AIRB regulatory approval
  • Simpler portfolios without complex risk profiles
  • Conservative capital planning
  • Compliance baseline requirement

🔐 Collateral Treatment & Credit Risk Mitigation

Collateral Adjustment Formula

Adjusted Collateral Value:
Adjusted Collateral = Collateral Value × (1 - Haircut)

Adjusted Exposure:
Adjusted Exposure = Max(Loan Amount - Adjusted Collateral, 0)

Standard Haircuts by Collateral Type

Collateral Type Standard Haircut Reason
Cash 0% Perfect liquidity, no loss potential
Government Securities 5% Sovereign credit risk, minimal price volatility
Corporate Bonds 10% Issuer credit risk, moderate volatility
Equities 20% Market volatility, higher haircut
Other Collateral 25% Conservative default haircut

Collateral Example

Secured Loan with Government Securities

Loan Amount: $1,000,000
Collateral Type: Government Securities
Collateral Value: $300,000
Haircut: 5%

Calculation:
Adjusted Collateral = $300,000 × (1 - 0.05) = $285,000
Adjusted Exposure = $1,000,000 - $285,000 = $715,000

Result: Capital requirement calculated on $715,000, not $1,000,000

⚙️ Calculation Formulas

Basic Calculation (No Collateral)

Step 1: Lookup Risk Weight
Risk Weight (RW) = Table Value based on (Rating, Category)

Step 2: Calculate RWA
RWA = Exposure × Risk Weight

Step 3: Calculate Capital Requirement
Capital Required = RWA × 8%

Complete Calculation (With Collateral)

Step 1: Calculate Adjusted Exposure
Adjusted Exposure = Exposure - [Collateral Value × (1 - Haircut)]

Step 2: Lookup Risk Weight
RW = Table Value based on (Rating, Category)

Step 3: Calculate RWA
RWA = Adjusted Exposure × Risk Weight

Step 4: Calculate Capital Required
Capital Required = RWA × 8% (minimum Basel III requirement)

Capital Adequacy Ratios

Ratio Minimum % Formula
CET1 Ratio 4.5% CET1 Capital = Total RWA × 4.5%
Tier 1 Ratio 6.0% Tier 1 Capital = Total RWA × 6.0%
Total Capital Ratio 8.0% Total Capital = Total RWA × 8.0%

Which Approach Should You Use?
